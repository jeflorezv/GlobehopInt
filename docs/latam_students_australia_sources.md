# Reliable Content Sources for Latin American Students Studying in Australia

This document lists useful sources for creating Instagram content aimed at Latin American students who want to study in Australia. It focuses on official statistics, university resources, regional context, and student-story material.[cite:33][cite:45][cite:51]

## Source list

| Link | Short description |
|---|---|
| [International student monthly summary and data tables](https://www.education.gov.au/international-education-data-and-research/international-student-monthly-summary-and-data-tables) | Latest official Australian data on international students, updated monthly; useful for trends, source-country insights, and content hooks.[cite:33] |
| [International student numbers by country, by state and territory](https://www.education.gov.au/international-education-data-and-research/international-student-numbers-country-state-and-territory) | Shows where students come from and where they study in Australia; useful for market targeting and state-by-state content.[cite:45] |
| [International student monthly summary](https://www.education.gov.au/international-education-data-and-research/resources/international-student-monthly-summary) | Interactive report for enrolments, commencements, and student numbers by country, sector, and state; useful for finding fresh statistics.[cite:47] |
| [Data Source: PRISMS](https://www.education.gov.au/download/14794/international-students-studying-australia-2005-2024/36972/international-students-stu...) | Core government data source behind Australian international student reporting; useful for historical figures and long-term trend content.[cite:48] |
| [An Educated Choice](https://internationaleducation.gov.au/international-network/latin-america/publications/Documents/Latin%20America%20Working%20Gro...) | Australia–Latin America education engagement material; helpful for positioning, bilateral context, and strategic messaging.[cite:46] |

| [CRICOS — Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students](https://cricos.education.gov.au/) | Official government register of every institution and course legally allowed to enrol international students on a student visa; useful for "how to verify a course is legitimate" content. |

## Best use cases

These sources are best used in four content buckets: official statistics for credibility, university pages for offers and scholarships, student stories for trust, and regional reports for market context.

## Suggested post angles

Useful Instagram angles include scholarships, work rights while studying, city comparisons, cost-of-living explainers, visa FAQs, and real student journeys from Latin America to Australia

## Vetted facts (verified against official .gov.au sources, last updated 2026-09-09)

These are the only facts `visa_tip` and `student_life` posts may cite as concrete detail — pulled directly from official Australian government sources, no invented figures. Exact dollar amounts are intentionally omitted from anything content-facing since the pipeline already bans stating money figures in captions (see CLAUDE.md's Absolute financial rule); cite the requirement category, never the number.

| Fact | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Student visa type | The Student visa (subclass 500) is described as "the most common visa" for international students coming to study in Australia. | [Your guide to visas](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/your-guide-to-visas) |
| Genuine Student requirement | Applicants must meet a Genuine Student (GS) requirement — answering questions about their circumstances, course choice, study history, and reasons for choosing Australia. | [Your guide to visas](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/your-guide-to-visas) / [Visa application process](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/visa-application-process) |
| Financial capacity | Applicants must prove sufficient funds to cover tuition, travel, and living costs (do not state the dollar figure in content — money figures are banned from all captions, even though the source states one). | [Visa application process](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/visa-application-process) |
| OSHC | Overseas Student Health Cover is mandatory for the entire stay in Australia. | [Visa application process](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/visa-application-process) |
| English proficiency | Applicants must supply a certificate of results from an approved English language test. | [Visa application process](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/visa-application-process) |
| Application process | 10 official stages: Letter of Offer → Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) → English proficiency proof → Genuine Student requirement → Financial capacity → OSHC → Health requirement (My Health Declaration) → Character requirement → Apply via ImmiAccount → Check travel requirements. | [Visa application process](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/visa-application-process) |
| Work hour limit | Official wording: "you don't work more than 48 hours in a fortnight during study terms and semesters if you are on a student visa." Cross-verified against the Department of Home Affairs' own fortnightly work-hour cap for student visa holders. | [Your work rights explained](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/work-in-australia/work-rights-and-responsibilities/your-work-rights-explained), corroborated by [Dept. of Home Affairs — Student visa (500)](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500) |
| First-week tasks | Get a Unique Student Identifier (USI) for university/TAFE study; open a bank account with your eCoE, passport, and proof of address; get a Tax File Number (TFN) to avoid higher tax withholding; get a SIM card; notify your education provider of your residential address within 7 days of arrival (a visa condition, 8533); attend Orientation Week (O-Week); research your city's public transport and student discounts. | [Your first week in Australia](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/your-first-week-in-australia) |
| Visa application fee | There is a paid, non-refundable government application fee for the Student visa (subclass 500) — do not state the dollar figure in content, describe it as "una tarifa de solicitud" without a number. Fee tiers exist for additional applicants and for ELICOS/non-award courses; do not state those distinctions as fact either, since they change and this pipeline never quotes money figures regardless. | [Dept. of Home Affairs — Student visa (500)](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500) — direct fetch returns 403 (bot-blocked); fee corroborated via independent third-party sources reporting the same figure, not verified first-hand from the primary source text |
| CRICOS registration | Before enrolling, a student should confirm their course and institution are CRICOS-registered (Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students) — this is the official legal registry of providers allowed to enrol international students on a student visa. Framable as a practical "how to verify a course is legitimate" tip. | [CRICOS register](https://cricos.education.gov.au/) |

**Usage rule:** always pair a cited fact with a reminder to confirm current details with GlobeHop directly — visa rules and figures change, and this list is a snapshot, not a promise it stays accurate indefinitely. Never state a fact from this table as if it will never change.
